# Next steps

Ideas agreed for future iterations, roughly in the order they could be built. None of these are started.
Some were inspired by *Puzzle Pirates* (bold art, crew-duty puzzles). They borrow the spirit only: no art,
names or puzzle designs are copied.

## 1. Uniform Storybook look (the "full makeover")
Storybook is the default theme; Dark Wood stays available through the "Style" toggle.
- **Bold, outlined art.** Thicker dark outlines and more saturated colours on ships, buildings, icons and
  sails, so everything reads at a glance.
- **Hanging shop signs.** Every port building gets a wooden sign with an icon (scales for the bank, a
  tankard for the tavern, an anchor for the shipyard). The sign and the building glow on hover.
- **Ribbon banners for headings.** Screen titles ("Marketplace", "Victory!") sit on curled parchment or
  cloth ribbons instead of plain text. Same ribbon on every screen.
- **Flag and crew emblem.** Pick a flag colour and emblem when starting a game. It flies on your ships,
  marks your ship on the map and decorates the journal cover.

## 2. Puzzle-based fights
Short, optional skill puzzles that make the player's own hands matter.
- **Gunnery.** In battle, click a cannon in the gun list to start a quick timing or matching puzzle. A good
  result reloads that gun faster; a poor one changes nothing.
- **Repairs.** A short puzzle during a voyage after storm or battle damage reduces the hull lost.
- **Boarding.** When an enemy is nearly sunk, offer a short duel. Win it to capture the ship (add it to
  the fleet, or sell it) instead of sinking it.
- Puzzles must stay optional: the battle keeps running on its own if the player ignores them.

## 3. Captain outfits
- Customise your captain with **hats, shirts/coats, accessories and a pet** (parrot, monkey, cat...).
- Bought in shops: the market (or a tailor) gets a second tab, **Outfits**, next to Goods.
- Stock varies per port, like shipyards and taverns, so rare pieces are worth hunting.
- Purely cosmetic at first. A later option: small perks on rare pieces.
- Built on the same layered-portrait system as the townsfolk (`npcparts.py`, `npc_draw`): the captain
  portrait becomes layered too.

## 4. Ship classes instead of a strict ladder
Today a more expensive ship is simply better. Make the choice strategic instead:
- **Classes with trade-offs**, for example:
  - **Merchant:** large hold, few guns, slow.
  - **Warship:** many guns, strong hull, small hold.
  - **Runner:** fast (better flee chance, fewer encounters), light hull, medium hold.
  - **Explorer or smuggler:** customs-check bonus, storm resistance.
- **Inherent perks per hull** (e.g. "+20% hold", "reload +10%", "flee +15%"), shown in the shipyard.
- **Fleet roles.** A fleet becomes a mix of classes (a warship escorting a merchant) rather than "more and
  bigger ships".
- **Distinct looks.** Each class or hull gets its own colour scheme (hull paint, sail colour, trim), so
  ships are easy to tell apart in battle and in the shipyard. Damage sprites follow the same scheme.

## 5. Moneylender consequences
Debt currently only compounds (10% a month) and lowers net worth. Options to discuss:
- **Collectors:** past a threshold, the lender's thugs ambush you at sea.
- **Seizures:** the lender seizes cargo when you dock at their port.
- **Lockout:** the lender's port stops trading with you until you repay.

## 6. Platforms
- **Web build.** GameMaker can export to HTML5 and GX.games, but the game loads its art, fonts and data
  from files at run time (`sprite_add`, `font_add`, synchronous `buffer_load`), which browsers handle
  differently. A web version means turning those into built-in project assets (sprites, fonts, included
  data) generated by `tools/gen_project.py`, then testing saves in browser storage. Expect a few days of
  work, not a rewrite.
- **More languages.** Copy `datafiles/data/lang/es.json`, translate the values, add the code to
  `i18n_langs()` in `scr_i18n.gml`, and check with `python3 tools/i18n_check.py <code>`.
